Maayong pag-abot!

Welcome! Hiligaynon is spoken by ~9 million people in Western Visayas (Iloilo, Negros Occidental, Capiz, Guimaras) and Soccsksargen. Start with Greetings, then tap any phrase to hear it.

Pronunciation tips

  • Spelling is phonetic: every letter is pronounced. Vowels are short and clear: a (father), e/i (between “eh” and “ee”), o/u (between “oh” and “oo”).
  • ng is one sound, as in “singer” — even at the start of a word (ngalan).
  • A hyphen marks a glottal stop, a tiny catch in the throat: gab-i (night), kan-on (rice), sin-o (who).
  • g is always hard (as in “go”).

The 🔊 voice uses your device’s Filipino voice when available — close, but a native speaker is the real reference.

      Interpreter settings

      How to use it with headphones: plug in or connect your earbuds, choose They speak Hiligaynon → I hear English, tap the mic, and keep the phone pointed toward the speaker. Each time they pause, you’ll hear the English in your ears.

      Good to know: phones don’t have a dedicated Hiligaynon speech model, so the app listens with the closely related Filipino model and then translates from Hiligaynon. It works best with clear speech, short sentences and little background noise. Speech recognition and online translation send audio/text to your browser’s provider (Google/Apple) and Google Translate. Bluetooth earbuds’ microphones can take over the phone mic — if it can’t hear the other person, use wired earbuds or the phone’s own mic.
